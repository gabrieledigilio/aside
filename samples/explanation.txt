Okay so hear me out, the idea is basically a marketplace, but for leftover hackathon hardware. Every event ends with boxes of sensors and dev boards that nobody takes home. Organizers have supply they don't want, and student teams have demand they can't afford. So we sit in the middle and we fix the liquidity problem. The trick is we don't hold inventory, we just match, so it's asset-light. We make money with a take rate, ten percent on each swap, and the average box is worth about forty euros. With two hundred events a year in Europe, that's the whole market. And there's a flywheel: more events means more boards, more boards means more students, and more students makes organizers list faster. For shipping there are two options. Either the organizer ships each box, which is slow and eats our margin, or students pick up at the next event, which is free but takes weeks. The plan is simple: first we launch here at Junction, then we sign five organizers, and by spring we open the marketplace to every campus. As you can see on this slide, the numbers work.
